Triadic rigidity demo

For any three points A, B, C in an ultrametric space, the three pairwise distances satisfy: the two largest are equal. This is the strong triangle inequality made concrete — no intermediate "long side."

Press Test Triadic Rigidity to verify with random triples.

The three distances

Cophenetic: the height at which two leaves first share an ancestor in the dendrogram — the tree's internal similarity. Used in hierarchical clustering.

Euclidean: the straight-line distance in the plane where leaves are drawn. It ignores the tree entirely.

Ultrametric: the maximum edge weight along the path between two leaves. In a tree with the strong triangle inequality, this is the correct "taxicab" distance of the hierarchy.

Triadic rigidity theorem

In an ultrametric space, for any A, B, C:

max(d(A,B), d(B,C), d(C,A)) appears at least twice

Every triangle is isosceles-with-short-base (or equilateral). The two largest distances coincide. This rigidity is what makes ultrametric geometry hierarchical: three points cannot form a generic scalene triangle.

Press the rigidity button repeatedly — the theorem holds every time, because it is a theorem.
